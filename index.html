How Australia Remembers

Over 100,000 Australians have died in war. Do the nation's memorials reflect that cost?

On Anzac Day, 25 April, Australians gather at war memorials. Memorials stand in towns, schools, churches and parks. Some list local names; others are a single stone.

Yet Australia's wars differed hugely in cost. The two world wars took 101,385 lives between them. Vietnam took 524; Afghanistan took 47. Do the memorials reflect these differences?

This story compares deaths and memorials, war by war. It draws on Australian War Memorial and Queensland Government records.

1The cost of war

The Australian War Memorial (AWM) counts Australia's war dead. Its table covers 36 conflicts and deployments since 1860.

2What Australia builds to remember

Places of Pride is Australia's national register of war memorials. The Australian War Memorial runs it. Anyone can add an entry, so it keeps growing. On 12 October 2026 it held 12,935 memorials.

3Where memorials stand: Queensland

Why only Queensland? The national register has no downloadable data. Queensland's open register lists memorial locations. So Queensland serves as a case study.

This chapter covers Queensland only, not all of Australia. Queensland's register holds 1,399 memorials, recorded up to December 2019. They appear in 66 of the state's 78 councils.

761 of 1,399 Queensland memorials (54%) honour the First World War.

4Do memorials match the cost?

This chapter returns to all of Australia. Compare each war's share of deaths and of memorial mentions. If memory followed cost, every line would be flat.

What the data can and cannot tell us

Memorial counts show how often a war is commemorated. They do not measure how important a war was.

The data cannot say why later wars are remembered more.

Remembering is a choice made town by town. It reflects the cost of war, but not only that.

Key terms

Anzac Day
25 April, Australia's national day of war remembrance. ANZAC means Australian and New Zealand Army Corps.
Australian War Memorial (AWM)
Australia's national war museum and memorial, in Canberra.
Honour board, honour roll
A list naming people from a community who served.
Cenotaph
A monument to people buried elsewhere.
Malayan Emergency
The 1948–1960 conflict in Malaya, where Australian forces served.
Indonesian Confrontation
The 1962–1966 conflict over Malaysia's formation.
Council
Local government for a town, city or district.
RAAF
Royal Australian Air Force.
